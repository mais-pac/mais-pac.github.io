Mathematics Fundamentals

The language physics is written in. This curriculum follows the same house style as our IOAA and IPhO sections — topic codes, grouped cards, lectures and worked paths — so you always know where you are and what comes next.

Why a math foundation for the club

Every formula in our Teaching sheets is a sentence in the language below. The IPhO syllabus quietly assumes Calculus 1–3 and linear algebra; the IOAA syllabus assumes algebra, graphs, logarithms and basic statistics. When the maths is solid, the physics stops feeling like memorisation and starts feeling like reading.

You do not need all of it at once. Pick the branch that is blocking you today — most club members begin with the Calculus pathway, because that is the gate to mechanics, electromagnetism, orbits and cosmology.

Direct comparison of core branches

Five branches, five different questions. If you can say which question you are asking, you have already chosen the right tool.

Branch Primary object of study Core question it answers Common real-world application
Algebra Equations and structures “What is the value of the unknown variable?” Computer science algorithms, structural engineering
Calculus Change and accumulation “How fast is this changing right now?” Rocket trajectories, economic forecasting, physics
Geometry Shapes and spatial relations “What are the dimensions and properties of this space?” Architecture, computer graphics, navigation
Statistics Data and trends “What story is this dataset telling us?” Clinical medical trials, market research, AI training
Discrete Math Distinct, separated values “What is the most efficient path or combination?” Cryptography, network routing, logistics

The five branches in detail

Club topic codes are for navigation. Cards marked Lecture open a full foundation page in this same style.

M1 · Arithmetic & Algebra — numbers and structures

The foundation: concrete values first, then variables, then abstract structures. Arithmetic is the counting and operating you already do; algebra replaces numbers with letters so one rule can answer a whole family of questions. Advanced algebra (linear and abstract algebra) deals with vector spaces, matrices, and structures like groups and rings — the machinery behind quantum mechanics and crystal symmetry.

  • M1.01

    Arithmetic & Number Sense

    Addition, subtraction, multiplication, division, fractions, ratios and orders of magnitude — the concrete values everything else is built on.

  • M1.02

    Algebra, Equations & Functions

    Variables (x, y, z), generalized rules, and finding unknown values. Functions map inputs to outputs — the first abstraction in physics.

  • M1.03

    Linear Algebra: Matrices & Vector Spaces

    Vectors, matrices, transformations. The language of quantum states, computer graphics, rotations and coupled oscillators.

  • M1.04

    Abstract Algebra: Groups & Rings advanced

    Symmetry made rigorous. Groups describe what stays the same when you rotate, reflect or reorder — from crystals to particle physics.

M2 · Calculus & Analysis — change and continuous motion

The mathematics of becoming: how fast something is changing at an instant (differential calculus), how much has accumulated (integral calculus), and the rigorous foundation underneath both — limits, infinite series, continuity, and the nature of the real numbers (mathematical analysis). This is the club's recommended starting point.

M3 · Geometry & Topology — shape, size and space

Geometry studies shapes, sizes, positions and properties of space — angles, lines, triangles, circles — including Euclidean geometry, differential geometry (curves and surfaces), and non-Euclidean geometry (used in general relativity). Topology, often called “rubber-sheet geometry,” studies what survives stretching, twisting and deforming without tearing or gluing: the number of holes, not the number of centimetres.

  • M3.01

    Euclidean Geometry & Trigonometry

    Triangles, circles, similarity, the Pythagorean theorem and the trig functions — the geometry of everyday space.

  • M3.02

    Coordinate & Analytic Geometry

    Putting geometry into equations: lines, conics, coordinates — the bridge that made calculus possible.

  • M3.03

    Differential Geometry & Non-Euclidean Space

    Curves and surfaces described by derivatives; curved spacetime where parallel lines can meet — the geometry behind general relativity.

  • M3.04

    Topology

    Properties unchanged by continuous deformation: knots, holes, connectedness. From coffee cups to the shape of the universe.

M4 · Probability & Statistics — randomness and data

Probability is the mathematical modelling of randomness and uncertainty: it quantifies the likelihood that a specific event will occur. Statistics is the science of collecting, organizing, analyzing, interpreting and presenting data — turning raw numbers into actionable insights and predictions. Together they are how science deals with noise, error and evidence.

  • M4.01

    Probability Foundations

    Sample spaces, events, conditional probability and Bayes' rule — quantifying “how likely?” before any data arrives.

  • M4.02

    Random Variables & Distributions

    Expectation, variance, the binomial, Poisson and normal distributions — the shapes randomness tends to make.

  • M4.03

    Statistics: From Data to Inference

    Sampling, error bars, confidence intervals and hypothesis testing — the toolkit behind every experimental claim, including IPhO data analysis.

M5 · Discrete Mathematics — finite and countable structures

Where values are distinct and separated rather than continuous: counting, networks and proof. Discrete mathematics is the working language of computer science, cryptography and logistics — and of clean mathematical reasoning itself.

  • M5.01

    Combinatorics & Counting

    Counting, arranging and combining objects — how many passwords, orbits or orderings are even possible?

  • M5.02

    Graph Theory

    Networks of points connected by lines: circuits, maps, social graphs, and the shortest path between two stations.

  • M5.03

    Mathematical Logic & Proof

    Truth values, implication and the forms of proof — the bedrock of programming and of every physics derivation.

Where this connects

With our IPhO curriculum

IPhO group 04 already carries the physics-flavoured version of this material: Algebra, Functions and Geometry, Vectors and Complex Numbers, Calculus and Approximation, Taylor Series and Numerical Methods. Use this math section as the groundwork, then move to those handouts for physics-styled problems.

With real research

Gradient, divergence, curl and the Jacobian are not exam trivia — they are how the papers on our Resources page talk about fields, flux and transformations. Read the Calculus III lecture, then open a real paper and find the maths you recognise.

With the sky

Orbits are calculus. Starlight spectra are statistics. Galaxy maps are geometry. Follow what is happening now on the News page, and check the club activities for paper-reading nights where we tackle this material together.
