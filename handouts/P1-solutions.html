Physics P1: Solutions

Physical Reasoning and Estimation · MAIS PAC

Problem 1.

(a) Write v = C λa gb ρc. Since [v] = [L][T]−1, [λ] = [L], [g] = [L][T]−2, and [ρ] = [M][L]−3, matching exponents of [M] immediately gives c = 0: ρ is the only parameter carrying mass, so nothing can cancel it! (Physically, doubling water’s density doubles both the gravitational restoring weight and the inertia of each fluid parcel, so the two cancel.) Matching [T] gives −2b = −1 ⇒ b = 1/2, and matching [L] gives a + b = 1 ⇒ a = 1/2. Thus

v = C √(gλ)
(1)

(exact fluid theory gives C = 1/√(2π) ≈ 0.40, so longer ocean swells outrun shorter chop).

(b) When h ≪ λ and v is independent of λ, the same reasoning with h replacing λ gives v = C' √(gh) (in fact C' = 1 exactly for linear shallow-water waves). For h = 4.0 × 103 m,

v ≈ √((9.8 m/s2)(4000 m)) ≈ 198 m/s ≈ 710 km/h,
(2)

comparable to a commercial jetliner.

Problem 2.

(a) Test each limit in turn:

  • 1. Flat wedge (θ → 0): the block sits at rest on a flat horizontal surface, so we must have A → 0. Candidate (i) gives mg/(M + m) ≠ 0, eliminating (i).
  • 2. Vertical wall (θ → π/2): the block drops straight down beside a vertical cliff without pressing horizontally against the wedge, so again A → 0. Candidate (iv) diverges (tan θ → ∞), eliminating (iv).
  • 3. Immovable wedge (M → ∞): an infinitely massive wedge cannot accelerate, so A → 0. Candidate (ii) approaches g sin θ cos θ ≠ 0 as M → ∞, eliminating (ii).
  • 4. Heavy wedge on a gentle slope (M ≫ m and small θ, with Mθ2 ≪ m): the block presses on the nearly flat wedge with normal force N ≈ mg, exerting a tiny horizontal force N sin θ ≈ mgθ on a massive wedge of mass M, so Newton’s second law requires A ≈ (m/M)gθ. Candidate (iii) gives mgθ/M as expected, whereas candidate (v) gives mgθ/m = gθ (independent of M!), eliminating (v). Only (iii) survives!

(b) Let the slope descend to the right and let the wedge recoil to the left with acceleration A. If N is the normal force between block and wedge, horizontal motion of the wedge gives MA = N sin θ. Because the block remains in contact with the wedge, its acceleration component along the outward normal (sin θ, cos θ) must equal that of the wedge, namely −A sin θ. Newton’s second law for the block along the normal gives

N − mg cos θ = −mA sin θ  ⇒  MAsin θ = mg cos θ − mA sin θ,
(3)

which rearranges directly to A = mg sin θ cos θM + m sin2 θ, confirming (iii).

Problem 3.

(a) Because the atmospheric scale height (≈ 8.5 km) is roughly 0.13% of R⊕, gravitational acceleration g is essentially uniform across nearly the entire mass of the air, and the spherical shell has surface area 4πR⊕2. Hydrostatic balance requires sea-level pressure to support the weight per unit area of the air column overhead: P0 = Matmg / (4πR⊕2). Hence

Matm ≈ 4π R⊕2 P0g = 4π (6.37 × 106 m)2 (1.01 × 105 Pa)9.8 m/s2 ≈ 5.3 × 1018 kg.
(4)

(Accounting for continental elevation lowers the true global value slightly to 5.15 × 1018 kg.)

(b) Local pressure measures the weight per unit area of the air above that altitude. Since PUB / P0 ≈ 8.6 × 104 / 1.01 × 105 ≈ 0.85, roughly 85% of a sea-level air column lies above 1350 m, and about 15% lies below Ulaanbaatar’s altitude.

Problem 4.

(a) We have [G] = [L]3[M]−1[T]−2, [ρ0] = [M][L]−3, and [R0] = [L]. Notice that [Gρ0] = [T]−2 already cancels both mass and length! Any factor of R0 would introduce unmatched length dimensions, so

tff = C 1√(Gρ0).
(5)

Physically, if we double R0 at fixed ρ0, each shell has twice as far to fall, but the enclosed mass M = 43πR03ρ0 increases eightfold so surface gravity GM/R02 ∝ ρ0R0 also doubles; from R0 = 12gt2, the two factors of 2 cancel! (Exact integration gives C = √(3π/32) ≈ 0.543.)

(b) The Sun’s mean density is ρ̄⊙ = M⊙ / (43πR⊙3) ≈ 1.41 × 103 kg/m3. Taking C ≈ 1 gives 1/√(Gρ̄⊙) ≈ 3.26 × 103 s ≈ 54 minutes (or 29 minutes with the exact prefactor √(3π/32)). The real Sun does not collapse in half an hour because nuclear fusion in its 1.5 × 107 K core maintains high thermal gas pressure that balances inward gravity at every radius (hydrostatic equilibrium).
