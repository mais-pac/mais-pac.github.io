Physics P2: Solutions

Motion, Forces, and Conservation · MAIS PAC

Problem 1.

(a) Conservation of mechanical energy between launch (height h, speed v0) and impact (height 0, speed vf) gives 12mv02 + mgh = 12mvf2, so

v1 = v2 = v3 = √(v02 + 2gh).
(1)

All three stones strike the ground at the exact same speed (though at different angles).

(b) Stone 1 rises from cliff height h and returns to cliff height h after time Δt = 2v0 sin α / g, at which moment its downward vertical velocity is −v0 sin α and its horizontal velocity is v0 cos α—identical to the initial state of stone 3 at the moment stone 3 was thrown! From that point onward, stone 1 traces the exact same descent to the ground as stone 3. Hence

t1 − t3 = 2v0 sin αg,
(2)

which depends only on the extra loop stone 1 makes above the cliff, not on h at all.

Problem 2.

(a) At the top of the loop (height 2R), gravity mg and inward track normal force Ntop ≥ 0 both point downward toward the center: mg + Ntop = mvtop2/R. At the threshold of losing contact (Ntop = 0), vtop2 = gR. Conservation of energy from rest at height hmin gives

mghmin = mg(2R) + 12m(gR)  ⇒  hmin = 52R.
(3)

(b) At height R (with h = 52R), energy conservation gives 12mv2 = mg(52R − R) = 32mgR, so v2 = 3gR. Therefore the inward horizontal centripetal acceleration is ar = v2/R = 3g, while the track wall is vertical so only gravity acts tangentially, giving downward tangential acceleration at = g. Since ar and at are perpendicular,

|a| = √((3g)2 + g2) = √10 g ≈ 3.16g,
(4)

directed inward toward the loop’s axis and downward at angle arctan(1/3) ≈ 18.4° below the horizontal.

Problem 3.

(a) Both balls reach the floor with speed v0 = √(2gh). Right after the basketball rebounds elastically off the floor, its velocity is +v0 (upward) while the tennis ball is still moving at −v0 (downward). Switch into the inertial frame moving upward with the heavy basketball (+v0): in this frame the basketball is a massive stationary wall (since M ≫ m), and the tennis ball approaches it downward at relative speed 2v0. An elastic bounce reverses its relative velocity to +2v0 upward in the basketball’s frame. Transforming back to the room frame gives

vtennis = v0 + 2v0 = 3v0.
(5)

(b) Since maximum height scales as v2/(2g), tripling the launch speed multiplies the height by 32 = 9, so the tennis ball reaches hmax = 9h (drawing the extra kinetic energy from a tiny slowdown of the heavy basketball). Spacecrafts perform the same trick gravitationally: swinging behind an orbiting planet is an elastic scatter in the planet’s rest frame, adding up to twice the planet’s orbital speed in the Sun’s frame without burning fuel!

Problem 4.

(a) Because the upper portion of the chain is in free fall (no tension acts between loose adjacent links), after descending distance y every falling link has downward speed v = √(2gy). In a short time dt, a segment of length dy = v dt and linear mass density λ = M/L reaches the pan, so mass arrives at rate

dmdt = λv = ML √(2gy).
(6)

(b) Two effects contribute to the normal force F exerted by the scale: (1) supporting the weight (λy)g of the length y already resting on the pan, and (2) supplying the upward impulse per unit time needed to decelerate incoming mass dm/dt from speed v to 0, namely Fimp = v(dm/dt) = λv2 = λ(2gy). Adding both contributions yields

F(y) = λgy + 2λgy = 3λgy = 3MgL y.
(7)

Just before the top link lands (y → L−), the scale reads 3Mg—three times the chain’s weight! Once the entire chain has come to rest on the pan, the impact term vanishes and the reading drops abruptly to Mg.
