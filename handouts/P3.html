Physics P3: Gravity, Orbits, and Waves

MAIS Physics & Astronomy Club (PAC)

Newton’s discovery that the same inverse-square law governs a falling apple and the orbiting Moon turned astronomy into a branch of physics. Together with wave physics and the Doppler shift, orbital mechanics lets us weigh distant planets, stars, and even invisible companions across light-years of empty space.

1. Universal Gravitation and Circular Orbits

Two point masses M and m separated by distance r attract with force F = GMm/r2, with potential energy U(r) = −GMm/r (choosing U(∞) = 0). By Newton’s shell theorem, any spherically symmetric body acts on external objects as if all of its mass were concentrated at its center. For a circular orbit of radius r around a much heavier mass M ≫ m, equating gravitational pull to centripetal force gives

GMmr2 = mv2r  ⇒  v = √(GMr),  P2 = 4π2GM r3.
(1)

[1] Problem 1 (Geostationary satellites). A weather or communications satellite in geostationary orbit stays fixed above a single longitude on Earth’s equator (G = 6.67 × 10−11 N·m2/kg2, M⊕ = 5.97 × 1024 kg, R⊕ = 6.37 × 106 m).

  • (a) Explain why the satellite’s orbital period must equal Earth’s sidereal rotation period (P = 86,164 s) rather than the 86,400 s solar day, and explain why a stationary orbit directly above Ulaanbaatar (latitude 47.9° N) is impossible under gravity alone.
  • (b) Compute the satellite’s orbital radius r and its altitude h = r − R⊕ above Earth’s surface (in km).

2. Elliptical Orbits and Orbital Energy

Bound orbits under an inverse-square force are ellipses with the central mass M at one focus. If the ellipse has semi-major axis a and eccentricity e (0 ≤ e < 1), its closest approach (periapsis) is rp = a(1 − e) and its farthest point (apoapsis) is ra = a(1 + e). Remarkably, both total mechanical energy E and orbital period P depend only on a, not on eccentricity:

E = K + U = −GMm2a,   v2 = GM (2r − 1a).
(2)

Remark (The vis-viva equation). The second relation in (2) follows immediately from E = 12mv2 − GMm/r = −GMm/(2a). Check two limits: for a circle (r = a) it gives v2 = GM/r; for an unbound parabolic escape trajectory (a → ∞) it gives escape speed vesc = √(2GM/r) = √2 vcirc.

[2] Problem 2 (The atmospheric drag paradox). A low-Earth satellite in a nearly circular orbit of radius r experiences a very weak atmospheric drag force opposing its motion, losing a small amount of mechanical energy per orbit while its path remains nearly circular.

  • (a) Does the satellite’s orbital speed v slow down or speed up as drag removes mechanical energy? Prove your answer using equation (2).
  • (b) Resolve the apparent paradox using the work–energy theorem: if drag opposes the velocity vector, what force does positive work larger than the negative work of drag?

[2] Problem 3 (Journey to Mars). Model Earth and Mars as moving in coplanar circular orbits around the Sun of radii r1 = 1.00 AU and r2 = 1.52 AU. A fuel-efficient Hohmann transfer places a spacecraft onto an elliptical orbit tangent to Earth’s orbit at perihelion (rp = r1) and tangent to Mars’s orbit at aphelion (ra = r2).

  • (a) Find the semi-major axis a of the transfer ellipse (in AU) and use Kepler’s third law (P2 = a3 in years and AU) to calculate the one-way travel time to Mars in days.
  • (b) Given Earth’s orbital speed v⊕ = 29.8 km/s, use the vis-viva equation to find the speed boost Δv1 needed to enter the transfer ellipse from Earth’s orbit (ignoring Earth’s local gravity).

3. Waves and the Doppler Effect

Light in vacuum travels at c = λν = 3.00 × 108 m/s. When a source moves with radial velocity vr ≪ c along the line of sight (taking vr > 0 for recession and vr < 0 for approach), every spectral line of rest wavelength λ0 shifts by

Δλλ0 = λ − λ0λ0 ≈ vrc.
(3)

[3] Problem 4 (Detecting unseen worlds via Doppler wobble). A star of mass M★ and a planet of mass mp ≪ M★ orbit their common center of mass in circular orbits of period P, viewed edge-on from Earth.

  • (a) Show that the star’s reflex orbital speed around the center of mass is v★ ≈ mp (2πGM★2P)1/3.
  • (b) Compute the Sun’s reflex speed v⊙ (in m/s) caused by Jupiter (mJ ≈ 9.55 × 10−4 M⊙, P = 11.86 yr, M⊙ = 1.99 × 1030 kg), and find the resulting fractional wavelength shift Δλ/λ0.
