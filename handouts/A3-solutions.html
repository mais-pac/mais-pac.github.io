Astronomy A3: Solutions

Relativity and Black Holes · MAIS PAC

Problem 1.

(a) For Earth, Rs = 2(6.67 × 10−11)(5.97 × 1024)/(3.00 × 108)2 ≈ 8.85 × 10−3 m = 8.9 mm—the size of a small glass marble! For Sagittarius A*, Rs ≈ 2.95 km × (4.15 × 106) ≈ 1.22 × 107 km ≈ 0.082 AU (comfortably inside Mercury’s 0.39 AU orbit).

(b) Nothing at all happens to Earth’s orbit! Outside any spherical mass M, the gravitational field depends solely on total mass M and distance r = 1 AU (Birkhoff’s theorem, the relativistic cousin of Newton’s shell theorem). Black holes are not cosmic vacuum cleaners: Earth would continue orbiting once a year at 29.8 km/s, though sunlight would stop arriving 8.3 minutes later.

Problem 2.

(a) Substituting Rs = 2GM/c2 into both definitions gives

ρ̄s = 3c632π G3M2 ∝ M−2,   Δa(Rs) = 2GMℓRs3 = c6ℓ4 G2M2 ∝ M−2.
(1)

(b) For M = 10 M⊙ = 1.99 × 1031 kg and ℓ = 1.8 m, Δa(Rs) ≈ 7.4 × 107 m/s2 ≈ 7.5 × 106 g0—tearing steel or astronauts apart (“spaghettification”) long before reaching the horizon. For M = 108 M⊙ (107 times heavier, so 1014 times weaker!), Δa(Rs) ≈ 7.4 × 10−7 m/s2 ≈ 7.5 × 10−8 g0, completely unnoticeable as you drift across the event horizon! Even more surprisingly, ρ̄s for a 108 M⊙ black hole is about 1.85 kg/m3—only slightly denser than room-temperature air.

Problem 3.

(a) Evaluating E = −GMm/(2r) at r = 6GM/c2 cancels GM immediately:

|ΔE| = GMm2 (6GM/c2) = 112 mc2 ≈ 0.083 mc2 (8.3%),
(2)

roughly an order of magnitude more efficient than hydrogen fusion (0.7%)!

(b) From L = η (dM/dt)c2 with L = 1013 × 3.83 × 1026 W = 3.83 × 1039 W and η = 0.10:

dMdt = 3.83 × 1039 W0.10 (3.00 × 108 m/s)2 ≈ 4.26 × 1023 kg/s ≈ 6.8 M⊙/yr.
(3)

Swallowing fewer than seven solar masses per year allows a single compact nucleus smaller than our Solar System to outshine a hundred Milky Way galaxies combined.

Problem 4.

(a) Since 1″ at 1 pc subtends 1 AU by definition of the parsec, 0.125″ at 8,200 pc corresponds to physical semi-major axis a = 0.125 × 8,200 = 1,025 AU. Applying Kepler’s third law in solar units (M/M⊙ = (a/AU)3 / (P/yr)2):

MM⊙ = (1,025)3(16.05)2 ≈ 1.077 × 109257.6 ≈ 4.18 × 106 M⊙.
(4)

(b) At pericenter, S2 sweeps within rp = a(1 − e) = 1,025(1 − 0.885) ≈ 118 AU of Sagittarius A*, proving that over four million solar masses lie packed inside a region barely wider than our Solar System (work recognized by the 2020 Nobel Prize in Physics). Combining the vis-viva equation vp2 = GM(2/rp − 1/a) = (GM/a)(1 + e)/(1 − e) with Kepler’s third law GM/a = (2πa/P)2 gives

vp = 2πaP √(1 + e1 − e) = 2π (1,025 × 1.496 × 1011 m)16.05 × 3.156 × 107 s √(1.8850.115) ≈ 7.7 × 106 m/s ≈ 0.026c (2.6% of c).
(5)

At 2.6% of the speed of light, relativistic gravitational redshift and Schwarzschild pericenter precession become directly measurable in S2’s starlight!
